differentials of inverse circular functions

If \(y=\arcsin x\), what is \(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}\) in terms of \(y\) ?

If \(y=\arcsin x\), what is \(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}\) in terms of \(y\) ?

\[x=\sin y\Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}=\cos y\]

If \(y=\arcsin x\), what is \(\cos y\)?

If \(y=\arcsin x\), what is \(\cos y\)?

\[\begin{aligned} &\sin^2y+\cos^2y=1\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\cos^2y=1-x^2\\[12pt] &-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq \sin y\leq \frac{\pi}{2}\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\cos y\geq 0\\[12pt] \Rightarrow &\cos y=\sqrt{1-x^2} \end{aligned}\]

What are \(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}\text{ and }\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of \(x\) ?

What are \(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}\text{ and }\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of \(x\) ?

\[\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}=\cos y=\sqrt{1-x^2}\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\\ \end{aligned}\]

Follow the same method to find \[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\arccos x\]

Follow the same method to find \[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\arccos x\]

\[\begin{aligned} y=\arccos x\Rightarrow &x=\cos y\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}=-\sin y\\[12pt] &\sin^2y+\cos^2y=1\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\sin^2y=1-x^2\\[12pt] &0\leq \cos y\leq \pi\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\sin y\geq 0\\[12pt] \Rightarrow &\sin y=\sqrt{1-x^2}\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}=-\sqrt{1-x^2}\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\\ \end{aligned}\]

Follow the same method to find \[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\arctan x\]

Follow the same method to find \[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\arctan x\]

\[\begin{aligned} y=\arctan x\Rightarrow &x=\tan y\\[6pt] \Rightarrow &\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}=\sec^2 y=1+\tan^2y=1+x^2\\[12pt] \Rightarrow &\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{1}{1+x^2}\\ \end{aligned}\]

integrals using inverse circular functions

Use the substitution \(u = \arcsin x\) for the integral \[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\]

Use the substitution \(u = \arcsin x\) for the integral \[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\]

\[\begin{aligned} u = \arcsin x \Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}} \end{aligned}\]

\[\begin{aligned} \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}x & = \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}u \\ & = \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}u \\ & = \int \mathrm{d}u \\ & = u + c \\ & = \arcsin x + c \end{aligned}\]

In practice, you might write the subsitution as \(x=\sin u\). Try it this way.

Use the subsitution \(x=\sin u\) for the integral \[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\]

\[\begin{aligned} x = \sin u \Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u} & = \cos u \\ & = \pm \sqrt{1 - \sin^{2}u} \\ & = \pm \sqrt{1 - x^{2}} \end{aligned}\]

At this point, it is really important to remember that the actual substitution is \(u=\arcsin x\) so that \(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u}>0\)

\[\begin{aligned} \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}x & = \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\;\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}u \\ & = \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}u \\ & = \int \mathrm{d}u \\ & = u + c \\ & = \arcsin x + c \end{aligned}\]

Use the subsitution \(x=\cos u\) for the integral \[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x\]

\[\begin{aligned} x = \cos u \Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u} & = -\sin u \\ & = \pm \sqrt{1 - \cos^{2}u} \\ & = \pm \sqrt{1 - x^{2}} \end{aligned}\]

At this point, it is really important to remember that the actual substitution is \(u=\arccos x\) so that \(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u}<0\)

\[\begin{aligned} \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}x & = \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\;\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}u \\ & = -\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}u \\ & = -\int \mathrm{d}u \\ & = -u + c \\ & = -\arccos x + c \end{aligned}\]

Explain why it is possible that both

\[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x=\arcsin x+c\]

and

\[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x=-\arccos x+c\]

Explain why it is possible that both

\[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x=\arcsin x+c\]

and

\[\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\;\mathrm{d}x=-\arccos x+c\]

Remember that

\[\arcsin x+\arccos x=\frac{\pi}{2}\]

so the two integrals differ by \(\frac{\pi}{2}\). That is, the "c" is different in each case.

Use the substitution \(x = \tan u\) for the integral:

\[\begin{aligned} \int\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x \end{aligned}\]

Use the substitution \(x = \tan u\) for the integral:

\[\begin{aligned} \int\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x \end{aligned}\]

\[\begin{aligned} x = \tan u \Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u} & = \sec^{2}u \\ & = 1 + \tan^{2}u \\ & = 1 + x^{2} \end{aligned}\]

\[\begin{aligned} \int\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}x & = \int\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\;\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}u \\ & = \int\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}\left( 1 + x^{2} \right)\;\mathrm{d}u \\ & = \int \mathrm{d}u \\ & = u + c \\ & = \arctan x + c \end{aligned}\]

Here, although the substitution is actually \(u=\arctan x\), there is no difficulty with the signs as there was before.

differentiating \(\boldsymbol{\arcsin x}\) from the definition

(a)   Use the identity above to find \(\sin\left(\arcsin(x + h) - \arcsin x\right)\) in terms of \(x\) and \(h\), without \(\sin\) or \(\cos\).

(b)   Put \(\theta(h) = \sin\left(\arcsin(x + h) - \arcsin x\right)\). Find

\[\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\theta(h)}{h}\]

(c)   Find

\[\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{\theta(h)}\]

(d)   Hence find

\[\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{h}\]

(e)   Now let \(f(x) = \arcsin x\). Explain why \(\arcsin(x+h) - \arcsin x = \arcsin\,\theta(h)\), and hence find \(f'(x)\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\arcsin x\).

(a) Find \(\theta(h)\).    (b) Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{\theta(h)}{h}\).

(a)   Applying the subtraction formula with \(A = \arcsin(x+h)\) and \(B = \arcsin x\):

\[\theta(h) = (x+h)\sqrt{1-x^{2}} - x\sqrt{1-(x+h)^{2}}\]

(b)

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{(x+h)\sqrt{1-x^{2}} - x\sqrt{1-(x+h)^{2}}}{h} &= \sqrt{1-x^{2}} + x\cdot\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}} - \sqrt{1-(x+h)^{2}}}{h} \\[6pt] &= \sqrt{1-x^{2}} + x\cdot\frac{1-x^{2}-\bigl(1-(x+h)^{2}\bigr)}{h\bigl(\sqrt{1-x^{2}}+\sqrt{1-(x+h)^{2}}\bigr)} \\[6pt] &= \sqrt{1-x^{2}} + x\cdot\frac{2xh+h^{2}}{h\bigl(\sqrt{1-x^{2}}+\sqrt{1-(x+h)^{2}}\bigr)} \\[6pt] &\to \sqrt{1-x^{2}} + x\cdot\frac{2x}{2\sqrt{1-x^{2}}} \quad\text{as }h\to 0 \\[6pt] &= \sqrt{1-x^{2}} + \frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}} \end{aligned}\]

(c) Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{\theta(h)}\).    (d) Hence find \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{h}\).    (e) Find \(f'(x)\) where \(f(x) = \arcsin x\).

(c)   As \(h\to 0\), \(\theta(h)\to 0\). Since \(\displaystyle\lim_{u\to 0}\frac{\arcsin u}{u} = 1\):

\[\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{\theta(h)} = 1\]

(d)

\[\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{h} = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\theta(h)}{h}\times\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{\theta(h)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\times 1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\]

(e)   Since \(\theta(h) = \sin\left(\arcsin(x+h)-\arcsin x\right)\), applying \(\arcsin\) to both sides gives \(\arcsin\,\theta(h) = \arcsin(x+h) - \arcsin x\) (the principal values agree for small \(h\)). Therefore:

\[\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\arcsin(x+h)-\arcsin x}{h} = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\arcsin\,\theta(h)}{h} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}} \end{aligned}\] \[\boxed{\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\arcsin x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}}\]

Well done on completing Part 3!

You have derived the derivatives of arcsin, arccos, and arctan, used them to evaluate integrals, and differentiated arcsin directly from the definition of the derivative.
