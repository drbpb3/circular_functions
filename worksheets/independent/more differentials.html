Differentials of reciprocal functions

What is the differential of \(\cos x\) ?

It's easy to see from the graph \(y=\cos x\) and the fact that it is a translation of the sin graph, that \[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\cos x=-\sin x\]

but we can also use the definition of a differential:

\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\cos x\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\cos(x+h)-\cos x}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\cos x\cos h-\sin x\sin h-\cos x}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{-\cos x(1-\cos h)-\sin x\sin h}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=-\cos x\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(1-\cos h)}{h}-\sin x\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\sin h}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=-\sin x\end{aligned}\]
Use the quotient rule to find the differential of \(\tan x\).
\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\frac{\cos^2 x+\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\frac{1}{\cos^2 x}=\sec^2 x\end{aligned}\]
Use the quotient rule to find the differential of \(\sec x\).
\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\sec x=\frac{1}{\cos x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\frac{\sin x}{\cos^2 x}=\sec x\tan x\end{aligned}\]

or if you prefer to differentiate everything using only the essential definition of a differential:

\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\sec x\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\sec(x+h)-\sec x}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\dfrac{1}{\cos(x+h)}-\dfrac{1}{\cos x}}{h}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\cos x-\cos(x+h)}{h\cos x\cos (x+h)}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\cos x-\cos (x+h)}{h}\cdot\frac{1}{\cos x\cos (x+h)}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\sin x\cdot\frac{1}{\cos^2 x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=\frac{\sin x}{\cos^2 x}=\sec x\tan x\end{aligned}\]
Use the quotient rule to find the differential of \(\operatorname{cosec} x\).
\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\operatorname{cosec} x=\frac{1}{\sin x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=-\frac{\cos x}{\sin^2 x}=-\operatorname{cosec} x\cot x\end{aligned}\]
Use the quotient rule to find the differential of \(\cot x\).
\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\cot x=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\frac{-\sin^2 x-\cos^2 x}{\sin^2 x}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\phantom{f'(x)}&=-\frac{1}{\sin^2 x}=-\operatorname{cosec}^2 x\end{aligned}\]

Well done!

You've found the differentials of the five reciprocal circular functions: \(\cos x\), \(\tan x\), \(\sec x\), \(\operatorname{cosec} x\), and \(\cot x\).
